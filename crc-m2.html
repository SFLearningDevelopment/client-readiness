Where a concern goes

Taking a concern to the wrong place makes a small problem larger.

Knowing where to take a problem is one of the most useful things you can carry into a client assignment. Taking a concern to the wrong place can make a small problem larger, can put SF in a difficult position with the client, and can leave you without the support you were looking for.

The escalation path

All concerns and escalations follow the SF hierarchy, in this order.

Step 1 — Account Manager

Your first point of contact for anything you cannot resolve yourself. The Account Manager knows the client relationship and the commercial context of your assignment.

Step 2 — Delivery Manager

If the matter is not resolved at the Account Manager level, or if it involves the Account Manager, take it to the Delivery Manager.

Step 3 — HR

For employment matters, workplace conduct concerns, and anything unresolved at the earlier steps.

The direct route to HR

Some matters are serious or sensitive enough that you should not have to work through the steps above first. Harassment, discrimination, bullying, retaliation and similar concerns may be reported directly to HR where that is appropriate or required. You do not need anyone's permission to use that route, and you do not need to raise it with your Account Manager first.

What goes to the client and what does not

This is the distinction to hold on to.

MatterWhere it goes
Task priorities, technical blockers, requirement clarifications, review feedback, sprint planning, timelines for the workDirectly with the client — normal working conversation
A complaint, a dispute, a demand, or anything with consequences beyond the immediate taskYour Account Manager first
Salary, appraisal, leave, assignment length, team allocation, a dispute with an SF colleagueSF only — never the client
Harassment, discrimination, bullying, retaliationSF HR, directly where appropriate
Never route these to the client

You should not approach the client's HR team or the client's management about your salary, your appraisal, your leave, your assignment length, your team allocation, a dispute with an SF colleague, or any other matter that belongs to your employment with SF. The client is not your employer and cannot resolve these. Raising them there creates confusion about who employs you and puts strain on the SF-client relationship.

Do
  • Raise concerns early, while they are still small.
  • Start with your Account Manager for anything you cannot resolve yourself.
  • Move to the Delivery Manager if the matter is unresolved or involves the Account Manager.
  • Go directly to HR for serious or sensitive concerns.
  • Keep discussing normal delivery work directly with the client.
Don't
  • Take SF employment matters to the client's HR or management.
  • Raise a formal complaint with the client instead of with SF.
  • Wait until a problem has grown before telling anyone.
  • Assume someone else has already reported it.

Module recap. Delivery questions go to the client. Employment questions go to SF, starting with the Account Manager. Serious or sensitive concerns can go straight to HR.
