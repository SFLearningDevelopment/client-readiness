Attendance, Leave & Performance Management

Which processes apply to your attendance, leave and performance while you are on assignment.

Two sets of expectations, both apply

SF process and the agreed client arrangement are not in competition.

On a client assignment you are meeting two sets of expectations at once, and both apply.

SF requirements cover your employment: how you apply for leave, how you record attendance and time, and where you raise employment-related concerns.

Agreed client working arrangements cover the assignment: the working hours, working days, site or remote arrangement, and availability that were agreed for your role.

Leave and attendance

Apply for leave through the SF process, and do it in advance wherever possible. Once leave is approved, inform the client team so your absence does not disrupt the work. Do not treat a message to your client lead as a substitute for applying through SF, and do not treat SF approval as a reason to skip informing the client.

For unplanned absence, follow the SF notification process and also inform the client contact for your assignment.

Record your attendance and time accurately and on schedule.

Employment-related concerns

Anything touching your employment — leave entitlement, attendance records, working hours, pay, appraisal — goes through SF, not the client.

Performance and client feedback

Clients will have views on your work, and that is normal. Where the client raises a performance concern, it should be routed through the Delivery Manager or Account Manager and managed internally by SF. SF, not the client, manages your performance.

If a client raises a concern with you directly, listen, stay professional, do not argue, and inform your Account Manager the same day so that SF can handle it properly.

Do
  • Apply for leave through the SF process, then inform the client team.
  • Follow the working arrangements agreed for your assignment.
  • Record attendance and time accurately.
  • Report client feedback about your performance to your Account Manager.
Don't
  • Arrange leave or changed hours informally with the client alone.
  • Take pay, appraisal or entitlement questions to client managers.
  • Argue with a client who raises a concern about your work.
  • Leave your Account Manager to hear about a performance issue from the client first.

Module recap. Follow the SF process and the agreed client arrangement together. Performance is managed by SF.
