Before you begin

This training is for SourceFuse employees working at a client location or on a client assignment. Its purpose is straightforward: so that you know what is expected of you inside a client environment, and so that you know where to take a question when one comes up.

Most workplaces run on an assumption so ordinary that nobody states it — the people who direct your work also employ you. On a client assignment that assumption stops being true, and almost every difficult situation on an assignment traces back to that one split.

There is more than one way a person can be connected to a client. It helps to see where your arrangement sits.

Your arrangement is the second one. SourceFuse employs you; the client directs your work. Everything that follows in this training is the practical consequence of that split — how to behave inside the client’s workplace, what you can agree to, and who to go to when something needs resolving.

1

Two relationships at once

Where you sit does not change who employs you.

When you work at a client location or on a client assignment, you remain a SourceFuse employee. That does not change based on where you sit, whose building you enter, whose badge you wear, or whose team meetings you attend.

Two separate relationships are running at the same time, and it helps to keep them clearly apart.

What the client does

The client directs your day-to-day work. They set delivery priorities, assign tasks, review the quality of what you produce, run the project meetings you attend, and decide what needs to be built or fixed next.

What SF does

SF owns your employment. Your appointment, salary, leave entitlement, appraisal, promotion, grievance handling and every other employment matter sits with SF, not with the client.

Why this matters

While you are on assignment, you are the face of SF to that client. The way you behave, communicate and deliver shapes how the client sees the whole company.

Remember
  • You remain an SF employee for the entire assignment.
  • The client manages day-to-day work and delivery.
  • SF manages the employee relationship.
  • You represent SF professionally at all times.
Do not assume
  • That sitting with a client team changes who employs you.
  • That the client can settle an employment question for you.
  • That a long assignment shifts the relationship.

Module recap. Client manages the work. SF manages the employee. Keep that division clear and most of the situations in this training answer themselves.
